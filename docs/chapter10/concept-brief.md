## Proposed learner sequence

Quick answer and five takeaways precede the numbered body.

1. What is internal vs external market structure? Plain definitions and immediate small/large nested diagram.
2. Internal vs external structure at a glance. Real HTML comparison table: reference scale, selected swings, role of a break, common error, limits.
3. Choose the reference range before naming the swings. Instrument, timeframe, session, last confirmed anchors, closed-bar policy and tie treatment; unresolved charts can remain unclassified. Distinguish a fixed demonstration convention from universal ICT rules.
4. How to mark internal and external structure, step by step. Five numbered actions and a distinct figure at each consequential step. Fixed references and knowledge available at each bar.
5. Can internal structure turn bearish while external structure stays bullish? Nested pullback example, and bearish mirror. Not every internal break is a larger reversal.
6. Internal break, external break or sweep? A stated close-based comparison, including wick-only excursion, exact-touch boundary and incomplete candle. An external break invalidates a selected map; it does not prove a new lasting trend.
7. The same swing can be internal in one map and external in another. Same-chart nesting and a deliberately chosen two-timeframe example; no universally best timeframe pair.
8. Worked example: the internal pullback ends while the external reference holds. Original synthetic OHLC path with exact prices, timestamps, confirmation sequence and captions. Chart replay should reveal evidence before the outcome; no prescribed trade.
9. Contrasting example: the external reference fails. Separate chart with the same initial context but structurally different continuation. Show when the prior interpretation stops qualifying; do not retroactively shift anchors.
10. When neither map is clear. Sideways/chop case, repeated internal breaks and legitimate uncertainty/stand-aside interpretation.
11. Structure is not the same as liquidity. Distinguish visible swings, inferred stop pools and imbalance terminology; Episode3 shows IRL is not exclusively an FVG. Link to Liquidity, BSL/SSL and FVG without expanding this into another liquidity chapter.
12. Common mistakes and myths. Tables covering hindsight, arbitrary re-anchoring, confusing three-bar geometry with importance, overfitting timeframes and assuming hidden orders or guaranteed continuation.
13. A practical chart-reading checklist. Short actionable checklist that produces a labelled evidence note, not a buy/sell signal; link to a relevant existing Chart Lab study without claiming it is a new dedicated drill.
14. Glossary. Concise topic-specific terms and definitions, not a repeat of Chapter09's whole glossary.
15. Frequently asked questions. Five candidates: difference between internal/external; how to select a swing; can internal structure change without external reversal; must they use different timeframes; is this identical to IRL/ERL? Visible native details/summary, smoothly exclusive, all answers in HTML.
16. Next steps and practice. Three applied quizzes (identify the map, confirmation timing, failure/uncertainty), contextual links, normal notes/navigation/review controls and one financial risk notice.
